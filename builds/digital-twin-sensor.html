The Point

The value is not more data. It is admissible context per unit of attention.

Bigger windows and bigger models help, but they do not solve the enterprise problem. The scarce resource is still attention: what the system is allowed to notice, what it can prove, what it must forget, and what should become durable institutional memory.

What The Sensor Sees

A workday decomposed into signals an agent can reason over.

01

Attention

Active application, focused window, dwell time, task switching, meeting overlap, and idle transitions.

02

03

Topic summaries, task labels, decision moments, uncertainty, interruptions, and recurring workflows.

04

Guardrails

PII masks, secret detection, consent scopes, retention rules, and policy before anything becomes memory.

The Pipeline

Capture less crud. Preserve more meaning.

  1. Collect

    Local sensors observe app focus, browser context, activity rhythms, and system events with explicit depth settings.

  2. Redact

    Names, cards, tokens, secrets, private URLs, and sensitive text are masked before storage or sync.

  3. Structure

    Events become typed nodes and edges: task, tool, artifact, person, meeting, decision, evidence, outcome.

  4. Admit

    Agents query a privacy gate, not the raw exhaust. Every answer carries source, scope, confidence, and expiry.

What The Twin Looks Like

A map of judgment, not a mirror of the person.

The dashboard is designed to answer one question quickly: what has the system learned about the way work moves? It shows attention, workflows, evidence trails, privacy blocks, and the places where the graph is still weak.

Research Spark

Inspired by the digital twin paper, but pushed toward deployable enterprise context.

The paper is useful because it treats the human twin as something that must keep learning. This build extends that idea into a product thesis: collect context continuously, preserve provenance, gate privacy aggressively, and let agents consume only the context they are allowed to admit into the task.
